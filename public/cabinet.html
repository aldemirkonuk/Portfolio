The line at the bottom

A live guess at whether you'll read to the end.

Starting point

Most people don't scroll far: Nielsen Norman Group found about 74% of viewing time stays in the first two screens. So I start you at 20%.

What moves it

How far you've come, how long you actually read (slow scroll, pauses), whether you skim (fast flicks), and whether you open things. Each nudges a logistic model, then the line eases toward it.

z = logit(0.20)
  + 3.4 · progress          // chapters seen, 0..1
  + 0.45 · min(opened, 4)   // panels, games, the lab
  + 1.2 · min(reading / 60s, 1)
  - 1.0 · min(skimming / 6s, 1)
p = 1 / (1 + e^-z)              // the line's length

Nothing is stored or sent anywhere. It lives in this tab and forgets you when you leave.

How far from home

There are 43,252,003,274,489,856,000 ways a cube can sit, and none needs more than 20 moves (Rokicki, Kociemba, Davidson and Dethridge proved it in 2010). Most sit at 18.

Up to 6 moves out, the distance is exact: I keep a table of every cube within 4 moves of solved and meet it halfway. Further out, Kociemba's two-phase algorithm finds a solution and I report its length, which is an upper bound. That's why far cubes say "about".

The live odds

Every position has 18 possible turns. Up to 4 moves out, I check all 18 exactly: closer, level, or further. That's the strip of squares, and you can click it. Across all 46,740 cubes within 4 moves of solved, the average is 1.1 closer, 2.2 level and 14.7 further.

You're modelled as skill plus chance: with probability s you find a closer turn, otherwise you turn at random. s is learned from your own turns, starting from a Beta(2, 2) prior. A Markov chain over distance then gives the chance you're home within 20 turns, averaged over every value s could take.

s ~ Beta(2 + helped, 2 + didn't)
step(d) = s·closer + (1-s)·[ 1.1/18 closer
                      + 2.2/18 level + 14.7/18 further ]
P(home within 20) = E_s[ P(chain hits 0 by step 20) ]
random turner     = the same chain with s = 0

Far out (17+ moves) the turn mix is extrapolated from the known number of cubes at each distance.

Distance, in code

T = everyCubeWithin(4)            // 46,741 states
dist(c):
  if c in T            return T[c]
  for m in MOVES       if c·m in T    return 1 + T[c·m]
  for m,n in MOVES²   if c·m·n in T  return 2 + T[c·m·n]
  return kociemba(c).length       // bound past 6

The solver runs in a background thread in your browser, so the page never freezes. Library: cube.js (MIT), after Kociemba.
